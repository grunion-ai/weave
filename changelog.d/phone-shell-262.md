- **At phone width the nav is a drawer, the page takes the whole width and controls hold a 40px hit box** (Issue #262): the 2026-09-12 audit opened the Issue table at 390x844 and 375x812 and found the workspace rail and the sidebar still in the layout at 316px together, so `#main` was squeezed to 66px (56px at 375), the crumb trail broke a word a line ("Weave / Workspace / Development"), the grid was out of reach, and 163 of 202 controls were under 32px. The drawer itself landed first on main as the shell's narrow-window rule (below 900px the sidebar is a drawer opened by the crumb bar's menu button over a scrim, below 600px the rail folds into it), so this change builds on that drawer instead of adding a top bar of its own. `wireNavHold` in `public/app.js` holds the page while that drawer is open, the way `holdPage` (Issue #263) holds it for a dialog: everything but the drawer and `MODAL_LIVE` goes inert, Tab and Shift+Tab cycle inside the drawer, focus goes in on open and back to the menu button on close, and the menu button carries `aria-expanded`. `holdPage` itself does not fit, because it inerts every child of `<body>` but its backdrop, and the drawer lives inside `#app` with the page it covers. The hold follows the drawer's `nav-peek` class, so every way the drawer closes (the scrim, its ‹, Escape, a hash change, a link followed inside it, the window growing past 900px) frees the page; Escape is taken in capture, so the dock and the grid behind do not hear it, and a desktop peek of a collapsed nav holds nothing. While it is open, Shift+Enter (quick-create), ⌘Z and `?` stand down as they do for a dialog: `#app.nav-held` joins `DOCK_ESC_OWNERS` and the quick-create listener's list, since a Shift+Enter in the drawer created a row in the held grid behind it. Under 768px (`max-width: 767.98px`) `#main` drops its floating margin and takes the full width (390px of 390), and the crumbs take the line beside the menu button with the view controls wrapped under them. A docked entity opens as a full-width, full-height sheet: `#dock` is fixed to the window at z-index 65, in the page's band of the stacking order (over `#hub-foot` and the selection puck, which drops to 62 at this width, and under the page loader, the drawer, the full-screen viewer, context menus, the tray, modals, ⌘K, pickers and toasts), so a whiteboard opened from a docked record paints over the sheet and the puck cannot act on rows behind it; the divider goes, and the 320px table floor from Issue #326 lets go; a stored pin is a flex basis, which a fixed box ignores. The desktop's `align-self: flex-start` shrank the fixed sheet to its content (483px of 844), because a positioned box honours `align-self`, so the phone rule sets it back to `normal`. The schema tray goes full width too. Buttons, chips and row controls carry an invisible `::before` box the size of the control or 40px, whichever is larger, centred on it, so a finger meets the control without the control growing. The box rule is a zero-specificity `:where()`, and a reference chip's link, which draws its kind glyph (`#`, `▦`, `◇`, `⬡`) with `::before` and its ↗ with `::after`, is left out of it: its hit box is its own vertical padding, given back by a negative margin, so the glyph stays and the line does not grow (an `:is()` carrying an id had outranked the glyph rules and blanked every `[[…]]` chip's glyph). Rows that stack with no gap (nav rows and space rows in the drawer, over the shell's 32px, and the grid's checkbox column) are 40px themselves, the `#` link's box grows from where its text starts, and the density switch rounds its end options instead of clipping them, which had clipped their hit boxes. On the same table, controls on screen with a full 40px hit box went from 1 of 42 to 26 of 45; the rest are the two crumb links, the column headers' hover-only menus, and controls cut by the grid's scroll edge or under the sticky add row. Above 767.98px no rule applies and nothing moves. `test/grid-window-browser.test.mjs` keeps main's 390px narrow case. Turning the grid into a Name and one-chip list on a phone (the first half of the Issue's second fix) is a design choice and stays open.
